# Pehla Din: Career Research (India, Nov 2026)

What Indian school and college students are pursuing, and the honest picture of each career: path, skills, pay, pros, cons, and scene ideas for simulations.

> Salary figures are compiled from salary sites, industry blogs and official bodies (ICAI, UPSC, NMC) as of 2026. Ranges vary a lot by city, college and employer. Treat them as ballpark numbers and re-check before showing them to users. Sources are listed at the bottom.

---

## 1. What students are actually chasing

### Demand signals (2026 numbers)

| Path | Signal | Competition reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Doctor (MBBS)** | 22,79,743 registered for NEET UG 2026 (13.3 lakh female, 9.5 lakh male); ~20 lakh appeared | ~1.37–1.41 lakh MBBS seats for 2026-27, so roughly 1 seat for every 16 registrants |
| **Engineering / Tech** | 16,04,854 unique candidates for JEE Main 2026, the highest ever | Fresher IT hiring fell from ~6 lakh (FY22) to ~1.2 lakh (FY25) |
| **Central university degrees (BA/BCom/BSc)** | ~15 lakh CUET UG 2026 registrations; top subjects English, Chemistry, Physics, General Test | Rising cut-offs at top universities |
| **Chartered Accountant** | ~10 lakh students registered with ICAI (2025 report) | CA Foundation pass rate ~19–21% per attempt |
| **Civil Services (IAS/IPS)** | 8,19,372 applied for UPSC CSE 2026; ~5.49 lakh appeared | 1,016 vacancies, so about 1 post for every ~800 applicants |
| **Law (NLUs)** | 92,344 CLAT 2026 registrations, up ~70% since 2023 | ~3,300–4,000 UG seats at NLUs |
| **Content creation** | Indian creators grew from ~0.96M (2020) to ~4.12M (2025) | Only ~8–10% earn a meaningful income |

### What Gen Z says it wants

- **Learning over money:** in Unstop's 2026 survey of 37,000+ students, 60–65% put learning and skill growth first when choosing a first job; only 11–13% put salary first.
- **Dream employers:** Google, Microsoft and Amazon top the list. Engineering students also like TCS, Infosys and Wipro, plus Swiggy, Meesho and Eternal.
- **Work-life balance:** Emeritus's 2026 Gen Z Outlook (ages 15–28, metros) found most prioritise balance over job titles.
- **Creator dreams:** a common view in the creator industry is that a large share of today's youth want to be creators.

### The big shift to show users honestly

Tech is still the most popular aspiration, but the *entry-level* market has changed. IT's share of fresher hiring fell from 32% (2025) to 24% (2026), while AI/ML job postings grew ~25% year on year (June 2026). Fresher openings are spreading to banking, GCCs, healthcare and sales. **Pehla Din's angle: show the job as it is in 2026, not 2019.**

## 2. Recommended launch lineup

**First 5 simulations** (largest number of aspirants and the biggest gap between hype and reality):

1. **Software Engineer**: biggest aspiration, most changed by AI. Also the one you can write from your own internship experience.
2. **Doctor (MBBS → Junior Resident)**: 22+ lakh aspirants, very long path, families pushing hard.
3. **Chartered Accountant**: the default commerce dream, with a brutal pass rate and an underrated payoff.
4. **Civil Services (UPSC)**: years of preparation for very few seats. A simulation of the *aspirant life* is as valuable as one of the job.
5. **Content Creator**: the new dream, with the starkest income reality.

**Wave 2:** AI/ML Engineer, Lawyer, UI/UX Designer, Cybersecurity Analyst, Digital Marketer.

## 3. Career profiles

### 3.1 Software Engineer

**Path:** B.Tech/BCA/MCA, or a self-taught portfolio → internship → SDE-1. 4 years of degree is typical, but skills matter more than the college for many startups.

**What you learn:** programming (DSA, one or two languages), system design, Git, debugging, reading other people's code, cloud basics, working with PMs and designers, and increasingly how to work with AI coding tools.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| Fresher | ~₹4–7 LPA at service companies; much higher at product companies |
| Overall average (all levels) | ~₹8.9 LPA base (Indeed) |
| Senior / top product companies | ₹20 LPA+, much more at top tech companies |

**Pros**
- Huge range of employers and remote options
- Skills transfer across industries; you can start without a top college
- Fast salary growth for strong performers; job-hopping is normal

**Cons**
- Fresher hiring has shrunk sharply; AI tools are taking over simple coding and testing work
- Constant upskilling; the stack you learn in college ages fast
- Deadlines, on-call duty and production bugs can eat evenings
- A lot of the job is meetings, unclear requirements and maintaining old code, not building cool things

**Scene ideas:** a vague ticket with no designs; a code review with 14 comments; a production bug at 7pm on Friday; a standup where you have nothing to report; AI wrote half the PR and now you must explain it.

---

### 3.2 AI/ML Engineer / Data Scientist

**Path:** CS/maths/statistics degree → Python, ML and SQL → projects, internships, Kaggle → ML Engineer / Data Scientist. Research roles often need a master's or PhD.

**What you learn:** Python, statistics, ML models, LLMs and RAG, data cleaning, experiment design, MLOps, explaining results to non-technical people.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| Fresher (0–1 yr) | ~₹6–12 LPA |
| 1–3 yrs | ~₹12–24 LPA |
| 3–6 yrs | ~₹24–42 LPA (more at top product companies) |

**Pros**
- Fastest-growing hiring segment even while overall IT hiring is flat
- Intellectually exciting; high pay ceiling
- Work spans every industry (finance, health, retail)

**Cons**
- Most of the job is cleaning messy data, not building models
- Fierce competition at entry level; strong offers often come only through internships
- The field changes every few months, so burnout risk is real
- Models fail in production in ways that are hard to explain to managers

**Scene ideas:** the data has 30% missing values and the demo is tomorrow; the model scores 95% in the notebook and 60% in production; a manager asks "can't AI just do this?"; deciding whether to ship a model you don't fully trust.

### 3.3 Doctor (MBBS → Specialist)

**Path:** NEET UG → MBBS (5.5 years including internship) → often NEET PG → MD/MS (3 years) → optional super-specialty (DM/MCh, 3 years). That's 10+ years to become a specialist.

**What you learn:** anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, clinical diagnosis, procedures, emergency care, talking to scared families, decisions under pressure.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| MBBS intern | ~₹24,000–30,000/month stipend in many government colleges (varies widely; some private colleges pay far less) |
| Junior Resident / Medical Officer | ~₹50,000–80,000/month in government roles (e.g., MP JR stipend ₹66,148/month from 2026) |
| MD/MS specialist | Grows strongly; super-specialists in private practice can earn crores |

**Pros**
- Deep meaning and social respect; you help people directly
- Very high job security; the doctor shortage keeps demand strong
- High earnings later, especially after specialisation

**Cons**
- Extremely competitive entry; many students spend 1–3 drop years
- 10+ years before stable, high income, while friends in other fields are already earning
- 24–36 hour shifts as a resident, burnout, and violence against doctors in some hospitals
- Private MBBS fees can run into crores

**Scene ideas:** hour 26 of a 36-hour shift; an angry relative in the emergency ward; choosing which of three patients to see first; your school friend posts their first car while you earn a stipend; NEET PG rank day.

### 3.4 Chartered Accountant

**Path:** CA Foundation (after Class 12, or provisional registration after Class 10) → CA Intermediate → 2–3 years articleship at a CA firm → CA Final. Usually 4.5–6 years in total; many take longer because of failed attempts.

**What you learn:** accounting, auditing, direct and indirect tax (GST), corporate law, financial reporting, Excel and Tally, client handling, deadline discipline.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| Articleship | Low stipend (fixed by ICAI/firm; much lower than a salary) |
| Newly qualified CA (ICAI campus, Apr–May 2026) | **Average ~₹13 LPA**; highest domestic offer ₹27.5 LPA; international offers higher |
| Experienced (consulting, banking, CFO track) | ₹20 LPA to well over ₹50 LPA |

**Pros**
- Strong, respected qualification; at the 2026 ICAI campus drive there were ~8,900 vacancies for ~5,800 candidates
- Good starting pay for the cost of the course (far cheaper than an MBA or private engineering)
- Paths into Big 4, banking, corporate finance, or your own practice

**Cons**
- Brutal exams: the Foundation pass rate is about 1 in 5; repeated attempts are common and demoralising
- Articleship is long, low-paid and often heavy on grunt work
- Audit and tax-season crunches (long nights before filing deadlines)
- AI and automation are changing routine bookkeeping and audit work

**Scene ideas:** the night before the GST filing deadline; a client hands you a shoebox of receipts; failing one group of CA Inter by 4 marks; your articleship boss asks you to stay late again; spotting something odd in a client's books.

### 3.5 Civil Services (IAS/IPS via UPSC)

**Path:** graduation in any subject → UPSC Prelims → Mains → Interview. Most serious aspirants prepare for 2–5 years; up to 6 attempts for general category (more for reserved categories).

**What you learn:** polity, history, geography, economy, ethics, essay writing, current affairs, and after selection: administration, crisis management, leading large teams, handling politics and public pressure.

**Pay (7th Pay Commission):**
| Stage | Pay |
|---|---|
| Entry (Level 10) | ₹56,100/month basic; with ~60% DA that's ~₹89,760 before HRA and other allowances |
| Senior | Rises through pay levels with promotions |
| Cabinet Secretary (top) | ₹2,50,000/month basic |
| Note | The 8th Pay Commission report is expected around 2027 |

Plus housing, vehicle, staff and job security, which is a big part of the real value.

**Pros**
- Huge impact on people's lives and real power to fix problems
- Unmatched social respect and job security
- Variety: postings across districts, ministries and sectors

**Cons**
- About 1 seat for every ~800 applicants; most aspirants never get selected
- Years of preparation with no income and big social pressure ("Abhi tak nahi hua?")
- Transfers, political pressure, long hours and little control over where you live
- Pay is modest compared with private-sector peers in the early years

**Scene ideas:** attempt 3 results day; a relative asks "what's your backup plan?" at a wedding; first posting as SDM, with a flood alert and a local politician on the phone at the same time; a coaching fee vs. family savings conversation.

### 3.6 Lawyer

**Path:** CLAT/AILET/state exams → 5-year integrated LLB (or 3-year LLB after graduation) → Bar Council enrolment → litigation, a corporate law firm, in-house legal, or judiciary exams.

**What you learn:** legal reasoning, drafting, research, arguing, negotiation, reading long documents fast.

**Pay:**
| Path | Reality |
|---|---|
| Top NLU → corporate law firm | Strong starts (e.g., NLSIU median UG placement ~₹16 LPA) |
| Litigation (court practice) | Often low pay in the first years while you build a practice under a senior |
| Judiciary | Government pay scale + security after clearing judicial services exams |

**Pros**
- Many paths: courts, firms, companies, policy, judiciary
- Intellectually demanding; strong long-term earning potential
- CLAT interest is growing fast (registrations up ~70% since 2023)

**Cons**
- Huge gap between top-NLU outcomes and everyone else
- Early litigation years can be financially tough
- Long hours at corporate firms; slow cases and paperwork in courts
- AI is changing research and document review

**Scene ideas:** drafting a petition at midnight for a 10:30am listing; your senior takes credit for your argument; a client wants you to bend the truth; your case is adjourned for the fifth time.

### 3.7 UI/UX Designer

**Path:** design degree (NID, NIFT, B.Des) or self-taught + portfolio → internship → junior product designer. A portfolio matters more than a degree.

**What you learn:** user research, wireframing, Figma, visual design, prototyping, usability testing, presenting and defending design decisions.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| Fresher | ~₹3–6 LPA |
| Early career UX designer | Median ~₹5.7 LPA (Payscale) |
| Senior / product designer at strong companies | Well into double-digit LPA |

**Pros**
- Creative work with a direct impact on how people use products
- Can enter without an engineering degree; remote-friendly
- Grows into product design, research or design leadership

**Cons**
- Crowded entry market after the bootcamp boom; a portfolio is everything
- Design by committee: stakeholders override your decisions
- Lower starting pay than engineering
- AI tools are speeding up routine UI work, so research and thinking skills matter more

**Scene ideas:** the founder says "make the logo bigger" on your 5th iteration; user testing proves your favourite design wrong; an engineer says your design is "impossible"; presenting to the CEO with 10 minutes' notice.

---

### 3.8 Cybersecurity Analyst

**Path:** CS/IT degree or networking background → certifications (CompTIA Security+, CEH, etc.) → SOC Analyst L1 → specialisations (pentesting, cloud security, incident response).

**What you learn:** networking, Linux, threat detection, SIEM tools, incident response, ethical hacking, security policies and compliance.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| SOC Analyst L1 (0–2 yrs) | ~₹4–8 LPA |
| Security Analyst (1–3 yrs) | ~₹5–10 LPA |
| Specialists / senior | ₹15 LPA+, with top roles much higher |

**Pros**
- Industry estimates point to a big shortage of skilled professionals
- Every industry needs it, especially banking (RBI flags AI-enabled cyberattacks as a top risk)
- Exciting, puzzle-like work for curious people

**Cons**
- Entry-level SOC work can mean night shifts and repetitive alert monitoring
- High-stress incidents; when something goes wrong, it's on you
- Never-ending learning and certifications

**Scene ideas:** 2:47am, 400 alerts and one of them is real; a phishing email fools the CFO; deciding whether to shut down a server during business hours; explaining a breach to non-technical management.

---

### 3.9 Digital Marketer

**Path:** any degree (BBA/BCom/BA/mass comm) + a course or self-learning → internship → executive role (SEO, social media, performance marketing, content).

**What you learn:** SEO, Google and Meta ads, analytics, copywriting, social media strategy, campaign budgeting, reading data to decide what to change.

**Pay:**
| Stage | Range |
|---|---|
| Fresher | ~₹2.5–6 LPA |
| SEO executive | ~₹3–8 LPA |
| Performance marketing (managing large ad budgets) | ~₹12–25 LPA |

**Pros**
- Low barrier to entry; any degree works
- Skills transfer to freelancing, your own brand, or startups
- Results are measurable, so good performers stand out

**Cons**
- Low starting pay in many companies
- Always on: algorithm changes, weekend campaigns, client pressure
- AI is automating routine content and ad tasks; specialists survive, generalists struggle

**Scene ideas:** the campaign burned ₹50,000 with 3 sales; Instagram changed its algorithm overnight; a client wants "viral" content by tomorrow; choosing between two ad creatives with real data.

---

### 3.10 Content Creator

**Path:** no fixed path. Pick a niche → post consistently for months or years → grow an audience → earn through brand deals, ads, affiliate links, courses or your own products.

**What you learn:** scripting, shooting and editing, storytelling, analytics, personal branding, pitching to brands, negotiation, running a one-person business.

**Pay (very uneven):**
| Level | Reality |
|---|---|
| Most creators | Little or nothing; only ~8–10% of Indian creators earn a meaningful income |
| Mid-sized creators | Irregular income from brand deals (e.g., one Kolkata creator shared earning ₹3.06 lakh from 5 brand deals in a month) |
| Macro creators | ~₹20 lakh to ₹1.5 crore a year; mega creators more |

**Pros**
- Almost zero cost to start: a phone and internet
- Creative freedom and being your own boss
- Creator jobs are growing too (Indeed: creator-related postings up ~919% from 2020 to early 2026), with roles like editors, managers and strategists

**Cons**
- Power-law income: a tiny fraction earn most of the money
- Unstable month to month; one algorithm change can wipe out reach
- Mental-health strain: comments, comparison, pressure to post constantly
- No salary, PF or paid leave; you're a business, with taxes and contracts

**Scene ideas:** your best video gets 312 views; a brand offers ₹15,000 for a product you don't like; a reel goes viral and you can't repeat it; family asks when you'll get a "real job"; month 8 earnings: ₹2,400.

## 4. Ideas for Pehla Din from this research

- **Add an "Aspirant mode"** for exam-heavy careers (NEET, UPSC, CA). The years of preparation *are* the job for a long time, and that's where most regret happens.
- **Show the 2026 reality for tech:** AI pair-programming, fewer fresher seats, internships as the main route in.
- **A "competition reality" card** for each career (e.g., "1 seat per ~16 NEET registrants", "1 IAS post per ~800 applicants").
- **Parents' angle:** most of these decisions are family decisions. A short "what your parents think vs. reality" section per career would resonate.
